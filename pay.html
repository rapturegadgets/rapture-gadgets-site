Fraud protection

How we confirm
a payment

Payment fraud works in both directions, so we protect ourselves and you. These are the rules we follow without exception.

  • We check our own mobile money statement or bank record. Money must actually appear in our account.
  • We never accept a screenshot alone as proof of payment. Screenshots can be edited in seconds.
  • We only send goods to the name and address confirmed in the order message.
  • Our staff never ask you to send money to a personal number. If someone does, it is not us.

Protect yourself from impostors

Only use the number printed on this website: +256 704 247 192. Anyone messaging you from another number claiming to be Rapture Gadgets is not us. If you are unsure, hang up and call the number on this page yourself.

If something is wrong

Our refund rule

We would rather give money back than sell you something you do not want. These rules are deliberately short so that you can hold us to them.

7 days, change your mind

If you simply change your mind, bring the phone back within 7 days with everything it came with. We refund the phone value, less any delivery charge. This does not apply to a phone with new scratches, drops or water damage.

Fault we missed

If a fault we did not disclose appears within 7 days, we repair it free or replace the phone. This is the promise that keeps us testing properly.

Repair work

If the same fault returns within 7 days of our repair, we look at it again at no charge. This covers our workmanship only, not new damage after you collected the phone.

What we cannot refund

We cannot take back a phone that has been opened by someone else, one that has new physical or water damage, or a special order we brought in for you at your request. Delivery charges are not refunded unless the mistake was ours. Full detail is on the Policies page.

Our reasoning

Why we chose these four methods

Mobile money comes first because Uganda does

Mobile money is how most Ugandans already move money. MTN MoMo and Airtel Money work on any phone, need no bank account, and settle in minutes. Offering them first removes the single biggest barrier between a customer with cash and a customer with a phone. We accept both networks because customers arrive with both, and refusing one would simply lose us the sale.

Bank transfer exists for our business buyers

Companies, schools and offices cannot pay a shop from a petty cash envelope. They need a bank trail, a quotation and a receipt that an accountant will accept. A bank transfer is slower, but it is the only method that a serious institutional buyer can approve internally, and it lets us issue proper paperwork.

Cash on delivery solves the trust gap

The biggest worry for a first‑time online buyer in Kampala is sending money to someone who never sends the phone. Cash on delivery removes that fear completely: you see the phone, you check the IMEI and the battery health, and only then do you hand over cash. It costs us more to run, and we accept that cost because it is what makes nervous first‑time buyers comfortable.

We deliberately left card payments out — for now

Card payment in Uganda has to run through a licensed payment provider such as a bank gateway, and it brings chargeback risk and extra fees that a small shop would have to pass on to you. For the value of phones we sell, mobile money already does the job better. We would add cards only if our business customers asked for them in enough numbers to justify the cost.

Would we accept cryptocurrency? No, and here is why

We would not accept Bitcoin, USDT or any other cryptocurrency as payment, and the reason is simple: it is not money here. The Government of Uganda and the Bank of Uganda have stated publicly that the government does not recognise any cryptocurrency as legal tender in Uganda. The Bank of Uganda is the only body allowed to issue and back Uganda’s currency, and it has made clear that crypto assets carry no such backing or legal privilege.

On top of that, the shilling value of a crypto payment would move between the moment we agree a price and the moment the coin arrives, which makes it impossible to promise a customer a fixed price. Until Uganda puts a clear legal and tax framework in place, we will keep taking the payment methods above.
